# NR601-001 — Four-Topic Midterm Resource Campaign

**Campaign:** NR 601 (Geriatric Assessment) midterm. **First asset:** the original Facebook video with Katz/Lawton hook, published by user, approx. four hours old at screenshot. **Status:** initial post published; follow-up assets drafted/planned only.

## Why this campaign
The student-facing product came first: a reported collection of approximately 17 documents and research resources was organized into **four NR 601 midterm topics**, then used with NotebookLM to create study notes, quizzes, flashcards, questions and audio or other multimodal learning material. The Katz/Lawton opening was a **single excerpt** of that wider preparation process, not the focus of the full product. See [product origin](./product-origin.md). The public caption includes a real response mechanism: email **Almanacstudy.support@gmail.com** with subject **NR 601 midterm**. It also invites students studying other courses to put that subject/exam in the subject line. This makes the campaign both a narrowly relevant study series and a method of detecting demand for other Almanac products.

## Learner and promise
- Core segment: students preparing for NR 601 midterm content spanning its four reported study topics; exact topic titles require source inspection.
- Educational promise: concise, accurate, original explanations and practice that help learners understand concepts, not memorize an institution's protected exam.
- Brand promise: `Know what to study. Be ready to show it.` Public-facing expression of the broader Almanac learning system.
- No promise of a pass grade, licensure result, school affiliation, clinical decision support or time-limited availability.

## Message architecture
- **Hook:** a real conceptual confusion or realistic study scenario.
- **Teach:** one useful distinction or decision principle; accurate without requiring a purchase.
- **Practise:** optional original check of understanding with rationale.
- **Invite:** offer a verified sample / answer follow-up or tell learners how to inquire about relevant additional resources.
- **Qualify:** if a student contacts the published email, ask which exact exam/course, date and preferred format, with consent; do not pressure or imply checkout is enabled.

## Product accuracy
Known product specifications include written modules, video overview, slides/visual summaries, explained original practice, flashcards, exam rehearsal and personalized plan. Do not claim every format currently exists for NR 601. The referenced full NR 601 midterm video is **not yet independently inventoried**, and its distribution rights, reviewer approval, price and availability are **unverified**. Follow-up CTA wording should say 'Ask about NR 601 resources' until those checks pass, while preserving the historical published caption as a factual record.

On 22 Nov 2026 at approximately 03:02 Nairobi (as shown on user screenshot): 360 displayed views and 4 shares; Facebook interface shows 4h since posting. Comments, saves, messages, email inquiries, contact quality and sales are unknown, not zero. Do not interpret this as statistical proof of conversion performance.

## Funnel and qualification
Facebook post -> subject-specific learning value -> email inquiry -> qualified request -> approved offer and fulfillment -> post-purchase learning continuity. Record counts and feedback in aggregate, not student names/contact addresses. User should always understand whether a resource is a free sample, a paid product or still under review.

## Operating constraints
- All nursing clinical material requires educational/clinical approval, specific topic and source review, rights clearance and an independent-originality check.
- No unauthorized institution-branded exam material, patient identifiers, or misleading 'official NR 601' claims.
- No active payment solicitation until current permitted receiving/checkout mode is evidenced. Last reviewed state recommended request-only.
- Posting, messaging, advertising spend and sale fulfillment stay with an explicitly authorized human or supported tool/action.

## Success signal
Evidence of qualified NR 601 requests is stronger than raw reach. Secondary success: constructive student comments, saves/shares, repeated visits to the series and interest in future courses. Review distributions at comparable post ages and iterate one variable at a time.
