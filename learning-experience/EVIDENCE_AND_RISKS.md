# Evidence brief and interpretation boundaries (accessed 22 Nov 2026)
This is a selective evidence record for planning, not a comprehensive systematic review or medical education recommendation.

## 1. Learning outcomes > attractive media
Mayer et al., *Multimedia Learning with Instructional Video* (Cambridge Handbook, 2021): effective videos follow coherence/segmenting principles and prompt self-explanation or retrieval. URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/multimedia-learning-with-instructional-video/ECE19433A23D0608BD6EE81CA7D224F2
**Decision:** prefer short, segmented video with targeted practice over passive lengthy promotional video. Note a 2025 meta-analysis reports heterogeneity/boundary conditions for principles; avoid universal oversimplification: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100730

## 2. Variety + accessibility, not unproven fixed learning styles
CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0 (2024): multiple means of engagement, representation, action/expression; specific media examples include speech, music, film, illustrations, comics and storytelling. URLs: https://udlguidelines.cast.org/ ; https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/expression-communication/multiple-media/
**Decision:** permit learners to choose a starting modality, keep learning objectives and valid checks consistent, and do not diagnose fixed 'visual/auditory learners'.

## 3. Retrieval and distributed review
Nature Reviews Psychology, *The science of effective learning with spacing and retrieval practice* (2022), https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-022-00089-1
Meta-analysis in authentic classrooms published 2026 reports average testing g≈0.37, spacing g≈0.20 and interleaving g≈0.40 across heterogeneous settings, with sparse boundary subgroups; do not promise a fixed effect for a new Almanac feature: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-026-10225-5
**Decision:** retain the current FSRS/retrieval investments as durable foundation; media is an adjunct and practice requires accurate feedback.

## 4. Games and gamification
A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis reported improvements in intrinsic motivation/autonomy-related outcomes but minimal impact on perceived competence in its comparisons: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7
A 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of randomized serious games in public health education found possible benefits but noted moderate methodological limitations: https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e89281/
**Decision:** games are best thought of as opportunities for meaningful decisions, feedback and rehearsal rather than a substitute for instruction. Pilot cheap and compare against non-game checks. Clinical simulations need substantial subject review.

## 5. Stories and cases
A 2026 realist review of case-based learning in health-profession education emphasizes contextual mechanisms and variability of implementations rather than one reliable universal format: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13350282/
**Decision:** story is a promising context for application, not just entertainment. Fictional cases must have clear debrief, feedback and transfer; no invented real patient histories.

## 6. Music and mnemonics
A 2024 systematic review of songs for language education recommends more robust intervention research and finds heterogeneous effects across outcomes: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0346251X24001325
**Decision:** use original musical cues as *narrow optional pilots*, particularly short ordered/vocabulary recall, not a universal learning method or safety-critical medical rules. Use a text/spoken comparison and a silent alternative.
**Warning:** evidence from young language learners does not automatically generalize to advanced nursing or adult exam candidates.

## 7. Multimedia accessibility
WCAG 2.2 https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag/ and W3C multimedia guidance https://www.w3.org/WAI/curricula/content-author-modules/multimedia/
**Decision:** accessible alternatives, transcripts, captions, text descriptions, contrast, keyboard support, no autoplay dependence. Some cognitive multimedia studies discourage redundant on-screen text; this does not supersede accessibility needs.

## 8. Current production tooling is an input, not a curriculum
Google 2026 NotebookLM update notes customization, Studio artifacts, slide/PPTX export and quizzes/flashcards: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/03/new-ways-to-customize-and-interact-with-your-content-in-NotebookLM.html
**Decision:** NotebookLM can accelerate asset drafting, but only reviewed sources, explicit rights, stable objectives and exact exported files establish commercial or published readiness.

## Risks, mitigations and decision
- **Media inflation:** many assets and no better learning -> cap formats by outcome.
- **AI hallucination or source leakage:** cross-check every generated claim, external license, versioned approval.
- **Clinical simplification:** do not teach actual patient care through unreviewed songs/games or reduce complex guidelines to false mnemonics.
- **Choice overload:** sensible default study path and optional format switch.
- **Distraction:** mute/disable music and animations, no leaderboard shaming.
- **Accessibility and bandwidth:** text-first alternatives and portable offline formats.
- **Copyright and voice likeness:** original or licensed music/images/voice, consent when person's likeness/voice used.
- **Measurement quality:** accept directional findings until controlled transfer/delayed recall measures are possible.
