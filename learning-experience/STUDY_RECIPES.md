# Almanac — Study Recipes and Learner Control v0.1
**Draft:** selected by goal and evidence, never positioned as fixed universal science or an immutable 'learning style'.

## Recipe A: Exam preparation
**When:** a student has an exam date and a finite collection of reviewed topic objectives.
1. Confirm syllabus scope, what the student knows and what remains untested.
2. Map 3–5 achievable focus objectives for the next session.
3. Choose an available starting explanation (read/watch/listen) or approved diagnostic quiz.
4. Attempt original retrieval and application questions, then inspect rationales.
5. Mark uncertainty precisely, revisit weak objectives and schedule spaced review.
6. Check on a new unseen item or applied scenario, and continue only with honest understanding status.
**User controls:** study session length, topic priority, start mode, order of approved assets, whether to try optional story/game/audio mnemonic.
**Protected:** coverage integrity; reviewed answer keys/rationales; accurate feedback; no fabricated promise that a topic is mastered because all videos played.

## Recipe B: Build conceptual understanding
**When:** learner says 'I don't get this process/concept.'
1. Ask for the concept and useful prerequisite.
2. Provide concise verified explanation and causal/concept map.
3. Show one original worked example (possibly animated/segmented video).
4. Ask learner to explain or compare from memory.
5. Correct an identified confusion, offer an alternate explanation if available, then pose a new transfer item.
**User controls:** visualization, reading, audio, pace, deeper example requests.
**Protected:** explanation/source accuracy, accessible text, actual transfer check.

## Recipe C: Recall and retention
**When:** memorizing stable terms or recalling knowledge already understood.
1. Confirm meaning before mnemonic.
2. Use approved flashcards with a genuine hidden response and FSRS due scheduling.
3. Optionally listen to original narration/rhythm/song if appropriate, licensed and user-selectable.
4. Answer a question without the cue; review forgotten items in next planned session.
**User controls:** optional mnemonic, quiet mode, start point, review target.
**Protected:** no song used as clinical decision guide, no corrupt FSRS due dates, genuine answer check.

## Recipe D: Applied decision practice
**When:** student needs to apply concepts in cases, problem solving, interpretation or decisions.
1. Introduce a labeled fictional scenario, constraints and an objective.
2. Let learner select a decision (low-fi PowerPoint story or simple browser game).
3. Give accurate consequence with explanation of why alternative reasoning may fail.
4. Present a *different* application problem; score reasoning, not entertainment points.
5. Offer recap and optional revisit.
**User controls:** story vs standard question format, complexity within approved scope, hints when supported.
**Protected:** no real patient details, no unreviewed medical care recommendation, no fake real-world decision outcomes.

## Recipe E: Busy learner / low-bandwidth study
**When:** user has short windows, commute, limited data, low power or accessibility needs.
1. Offer accessible text/low-data overview and an optional short audio segment with transcript if already available.
2. Set one manageable objective per session.
3. Use a brief independent spoken/written retrieval attempt.
4. Give explanation and choose a later revisit from real due/review evidence.
**User controls:** duration, silence, download where entitled, preference changes.
**Protected:** no forced heavy video or music, accessibility and factual parity across modes.

## Decision matrix
| Reported need | Suggested default | Alternative that learner can choose | Indicator of actual progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| 'Don't know what to study' | scope map + near-term plan | syllabus browsing | learner identifies objective & next step |
| 'Don't understand' | readable worked explanation | segmented video/diagram | explain a new example |
| 'Keep forgetting' | retrieval + spaced review | original mnemonic/audio adjunct | delayed unaided recall |
| 'Can recall but cannot apply' | novel original case/question with feedback | story/game pilot | independent transfer |
| 'Have little time/data' | short text-first lesson + question | optional audio | successfully finished meaningful check |
| 'Want creative learning' | optional story/game/music based on objective fit | ordinary explanation/quiz | equal or improved understanding, not just fun |

## Choice-budget defaults
Default to 3 visible study actions after login: **Continue recommended**, **Choose a topic**, **Review what I missed**. Advanced options hidden behind an explicit 'Customize my study path' action. Offer 4 starting modes (balanced/read/watch/practice) that mirror the current `PREFERENCES`; audio-first only when an actual usable asset exists. Avoid adding an extensive format-price shopping mall at onboarding.

## Learning outcomes states
Track separately: (1) content engaged, (2) independent practice attempted, (3) answer rationale reviewed, (4) evidence of understanding, (5) transfer/delayed recall if appropriately measured. 'Mastery' requires a reviewed valid standard and should not be inferred from a certain minutes-watched or media selected count. A user may skip a preferred modality, but no forced claim of mastery follows.

## Product feedback loop
Aggregate which routes users select or override, optional media usage, retries, targeted misconception tags and qualified format requests. Measure usefulness and task effort, subject to privacy. A learner's choices guide the product roadmap; do not treat one user's preference as a permanent universal classification.

## What could be built first
A single course-level 'Change how I study' panel implementing start mode, session length, topic emphasis, low-bandwidth selection, optional experimental media toggles (only when authorized/available), plus a recommended session composition. This requires secure backend state and actual available-media inventory before deployment.
