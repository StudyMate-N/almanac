# Pilot learning object: The Explain Test
**Status:** COMPLETE SCRIPT / PREPRODUCTION SPECIFICATION, no audio or video rendered here.
**Subject:** General academic study skills (not NR 601 clinical content); reduces source dependency while testing the system.
**Objective:** After a short practice session, learner identifies which concept to review next by explaining a chosen answer and contrasting at least one alternative.
**Core teaching proposition:** Correct recognition is not by itself evidence of an explainable understanding. Use prediction, reasoning and a gap-to-next-step decision.
**Limitation:** Not every exam question supports prediction before viewing options; adapt to format; no guarantee of exam score.

## Master approved-content candidate (editorial review required)
"Before revealing the answer, take a moment to predict what you expect. After choosing an option, state one reason the answer fits, then identify one reason another option does not. If you cannot explain a choice, mark the concept and revisit it with a clearer explanation or a worked example. End by trying an original similar problem without your notes."

## B. Private 2–3 minute audio companion (script)
"Take a practice question. Before uncovering the options, pause and say what you expect the answer to be. Now select your answer. Put the question aside, and explain why you chose it—as if you were teaching another person. Choose one alternative and explain why it doesn't fit. If the explanation is shaky, don't count the concept as mastered yet. Make one short note: what exactly is uncertain? Find a clear explanation, then attempt another original question without looking at your notes. This isn't about making every answer longer. It's about noticing which ideas you can use and which need another pass."
**Check:** learner reports one misconception and independently answers a comparable question. Transcript matches final audio.

## C. Editable PPTX/slide-lesson outline (7 slides)

## D. Low-fi branching story (PPTX links or simple HTML)
**Story premise:** A learner called Sam correctly guesses the answer to a practice question but cannot explain it.
- Choice A "Move on because it was right." -> Consequence: progress feels fast but the explanation remains untested. Debrief: correctness on one attempt may not establish transferable understanding.
- Choice B "Review every page from the beginning." -> Consequence: time spent is high; target not identified. Debrief: revision works better with an identifiable uncertainty.
- Choice C "Explain the correct choice and contrast another, then revisit the gap." -> Consequence: reveals precise uncertainty. Debrief: targeted retrieval and feedback supply a next step.
**Follow-up transfer question:** After a new practice item, which one-sentence explanation would distinguish two plausible choices? Use a genuinely new stem; assess the reasoning, not selection of C.

## E. Low-fi 'sorting' microgame
Give 6 independent study actions to classify as `Checks understanding` or `Mostly re-exposes material`. Examples: explaining an answer from memory, solving a new analogous problem, replaying an entire video, rereading highlighted pages, contrasting two solutions, repeating an answer explanation verbatim while looking at it. Avoid simplistic absolute claims: some rereading/listening is helpful for initial understanding; they simply don't independently establish retrieval ability.
- Support tap-to-select, keyboard, no timer, explanations and at least one transfer item.
- Compare against ordinary multiple-choice explanation; a game is not automatically more effective.

## F. Optional original rhythmic cue (no music generated in this milestone)
Four key actions: `Predict / Explain / Compare / Review`. Could be tried as a short original rhythm or sung cue but **do not** market it as a memory breakthrough. An accessible text version and a silent learning path are required. Compare it against spoken text recall; avoid using familiar copyrighted tunes.

## Observable outcome
Learning signal = ability to select the next useful study action and independently explain a new question after review, not video views/audio plays/game taps.
Cohort size, time delay and validity of scoring rubric are determined before any efficacy claim. If there is insufficient data, result = inconclusive.
